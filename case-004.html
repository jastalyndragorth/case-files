Initial Field Briefing

CASE 004 • ACTIVE INVESTIGATION

At 6:40 A.M. on Monday, police entered the Harrow family home after seventeen-year-old Noah Harrow failed to arrive at school and his mother reported him missing. There were no signs of forced entry and no evidence that Noah packed a bag.

The strange part is not that Noah vanished. It is that everyone in the house remembers someone else being there the night before.

Noah's mother says she set dinner for five. His older sister insists there were only four people home. Their neighbor heard a child's voice from an upstairs bedroom that has been empty for nine years. In the dining room, police photographed five place settings—and one chair pulled back from the table.

Hunter Objective: Find Noah, determine who—or what—the fifth place was for, and explain why the witnesses cannot agree on who was in the house.

Evidence

RECOVERED MATERIAL

Item A — Dining Room

Five plates were used. Four contain recognizable dinner remnants. The fifth was scraped almost perfectly clean. A water glass at that place has a small handprint on the outside.

Item B — Hallway Recording

A smart doorbell microphone captured three seconds of muffled conversation through an open window at 8:16 P.M. Two adult voices and two older children are identifiable. A fifth, higher voice says something too indistinct to transcribe.

Item C — Noah's Room

Noah's bed was slept in. His shoes, phone charger, wallet, and school bag remain. A framed family photograph on his desk has been turned face-down.

Item D — Upstairs Dust

Dust in the long-unused nursery shows a narrow disturbed path from the doorway to the window. The window itself was latched from inside.

Witness Statements

INTERVIEW EXTRACTS

Lena Harrow — Mother

Lena remembers setting a fifth place because Noah asked her to. She cannot remember who sat there. When pressed, she becomes certain it was 'a little girl' and then immediately says the idea is ridiculous.

Tess Harrow — Sister

Tess remembers arguing with Noah after dinner. She says he accused the family of pretending not to see someone standing in the hall. Tess saw nobody.

Marcus Reed — Neighbor

At about 9:00 P.M., Marcus heard a young child laughing upstairs while taking out his trash. He assumed relatives were visiting. He saw no unfamiliar car.

Officer Vale — First Response

Vale says the house felt normal until she entered the unused nursery. There she experienced a sudden, vivid memory of searching for a red mitten as a child. Her mother later confirmed no such incident ever happened.

Locations

FIELD NOTES

Harrow House

Built in 1926 and extensively renovated. The Harrows have lived there eleven years. No prior police calls involving violence or missing persons.

Unused Nursery

The room belonged to Noah's younger sister, Emily, who died in infancy nine years ago. It has mostly been used for storage since.

Back Garden

A narrow garden borders the nursery wall. No useful footprints were recovered after overnight rain. One old iron decorative trellis leans against the brick.

Archive Documents

RESEARCH EXTRACTS

Family Photo Album

Recent albums consistently show Lena, her husband Aaron, Noah, and Tess. Older albums include infant Emily. In one photograph taken shortly before Emily's death, an unfamiliar child's reflection appears in a dark television screen; the photographer cannot be identified.

County Archive — 1964

A family living at the same address reported their eight-year-old son missing for two days. He was found asleep in the attic. He repeatedly asked why his sister 'went away again.' Records show he was an only child.

Renovation Invoice — 2015

Workers opened a sealed wall cavity beside the nursery and removed old toys, children's drawings, and a rusted iron horseshoe. The contractor noted that the homeowners asked him to discard everything.

New Leads

SECOND-PASS REVIEW

1. Build at least three theories from the evidence. A haunting explains the nursery and family history. A creature that manipulates memory could explain the contradictory witnesses. An intruder or imitator could explain the extra place setting.

2. Notice what the case does NOT establish: nobody clearly saw the fifth person. Most testimony concerns memories, sounds, or expectations.

3. Compare incidents separated by decades. The recurring pattern is not simply disappearance; it is the sudden conviction that the family includes a child who should not exist.

4. Review the renovation record only after forming a theory. Ask whether removing an old object might matter more than the death associated with the room.

Hunter's Call: Do not identify the threat from a single clue. Explain the complete chain of events before filing your report.
